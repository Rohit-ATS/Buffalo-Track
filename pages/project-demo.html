The problem

A diagnosis should not begin with isolation.

1

Information is fragmented

Families must navigate medical language, studies, registries, foundations, and social groups across disconnected sources.

2

A useful connection needs a reason, a source, consent, and a safety boundary—not an opaque recommendation.

3

Privacy is essential

Medical details and contact information cannot be prerequisites for finding meaningful peer support.

Demo walkthrough

One safe vertical slice, end to end.

  1. Sign in with a passwordless Supabase email magic link.
  2. Onboard once: condition, patient/caregiver role, age band, time zone, language, help needed, and matching consent.
  3. Enter the role-specific dashboard. A family sees connections; an evidence reviewer sees sources; a steward sees Circle safety tools.
  4. Open a match. “Why Atlas suggested this” shows shared diagnosis, mechanism, life stage, research question, or resource need.
  5. Request, don’t expose. A private introduction request shares a note—not email, phone, or medical record.
  6. Accept privately. Mutual acceptance opens a private conversation; a steward can moderate a Circle and members can report or block.

Technical walkthrough

Trust is built into the architecture.

Supabase Auth + RLS

Magic-link authentication. Profiles, requests, messages, reports, and blocks are row-level protected. The browser only receives the anon key.

Private network model

profiles, circles, introduction_requests, private conversations, messages, and reports establish consent before contact.

Evidence layer

Evidence reviewers can inspect receipts. Family-facing cards receive approved public context and a clear source—not raw, unverified web results.

Bright Data ingestion

Server-side only. Bright Data discovers public, permitted organization and study pages; the pipeline records provenance, verifies claims, and caps spend.
